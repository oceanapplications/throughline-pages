eSIM or VPN for China? You probably want both.

travelersvpn.com guides

Short answer: they solve different halves of the same problem. A roaming eSIM routes your cellular data through gateways outside the firewall, so Instagram, WhatsApp, and Gmail work on mobile data with no VPN. But the moment you join hotel, cafe, or high-speed-rail wifi, the firewall is back, and that's the half a VPN covers. Both must be set up before you land.

How the eSIM loophole actually works

When you roam, your data traffic is tunneled back to your home carrier's (or the eSIM provider's) gateway before it reaches the internet. Travel eSIMs for China typically break out through Hong Kong or Singapore, which sit outside the Great Firewall. From the censors' perspective your traffic exits abroad, so blocked apps simply work on cellular. No VPN, no setup beyond installing the eSIM profile.

It's a genuinely good deal for a trip. The catches are real, though:

  • Wifi kills it. The loophole only exists on cellular. Join any mainland wifi network to save data or get better speed (hotel evenings, cafes, trains) and you're behind the firewall again with no protection at all.
  • Data-only. Travel eSIMs almost never include a phone number or SMS, so verification codes from Chinese services (WeChat, Alipay) can't arrive on them. Keep your home SIM active for SMS.
  • Cost per gigabyte. Unlimited China eSIMs run roughly $4 to $5 per day; capped plans are cheaper but streaming and video calls burn them fast. Heavy usage belongs on wifi, which is exactly where the eSIM can't help you.
  • Install before you fly. eSIM profiles generally can't be downloaded from inside China, same as VPN apps.

Frequently asked

Can I skip the VPN if I have an unlimited eSIM?

Only if you'll never touch wifi and don't mind cellular speeds for everything, including evening video calls at the hotel. Most travelers end up on wifi daily, and that's where an eSIM leaves you fully firewalled. The $9.99 pass exists for exactly that gap.

Can I skip the eSIM if I have a VPN?

Yes, more plausibly than the reverse: a local SIM or your home carrier's roaming plus the VPN covers everything. The eSIM's advantages are convenience and price on cellular data, not capability.
