Does Google Maps work in China?

travelersvpn.com guides

No, and here's the part most VPN sites won't tell you: a VPN doesn't really fix it. Google Maps is blocked in mainland China, and even with a working VPN its China map data is offset by roughly 50 to 500 meters, with stale or missing businesses and transit. Use Apple Maps instead; it works correctly in China without any VPN.

Two separate problems, and a VPN only solves one

Problem one: the block. Google's services have been blocked in mainland China since 2014, so on any mainland network Google Maps simply won't load. A VPN solves this part; the app opens and tiles appear.

Problem two: the data. China mandates a special coordinate system (GCJ-02) for map providers, and Google, having no license to operate there, publishes China map data that's misaligned with real GPS by roughly 50 to 500 meters. Your blue dot floats in the wrong street. On top of that, because Google can't collect data on the ground, its business listings, transit routes, and imagery for China are years stale. No VPN can fix any of that; the tunnel changes where your traffic goes, not what Google knows about Chengdu.

The practical result: travelers who "solved" Google Maps with a VPN follow walking directions into walls and search for restaurants that closed in 2019.

The actual fix: Apple Maps. Apple licenses its China data from Amap (Gaode), China's dominant mapping provider, so locations are accurate, listings are current, and it works with no VPN at all. If you have an iPhone, you already carry the best foreign-friendly map of China.

Your map options in China, ranked

  • Apple Maps. Accurate, English interface, no VPN needed. The default answer for iPhone travelers.
  • Amap (高德地图) or Baidu Maps. What locals use; the most complete data and live transit, but Chinese-language interfaces. Worth installing for metro navigation if you can manage the UI.
  • Google Maps + VPN. Fine for looking up saved places, reviews, and anything outside mainland China (your Tokyo layover, your flight home). Don't trust its blue dot or walking directions inside the mainland.

So why carry a VPN at all?

Because maps are the exception, not the rule. Instagram, WhatsApp, Gmail, YouTube, and Google Search are blocked outright and work perfectly through a tunnel; those are the apps a VPN is actually for. The trap with ordinary VPNs is that while the tunnel is up, everything routes through it, including Apple Maps, Didi, and your payment apps, which makes the local half of your phone slow or confused abroad.

Frequently asked

Does Google Maps work in China with a VPN?

It loads, but it doesn't work well. The block is lifted, yet Google's China map data remains offset by 50 to 500 meters with stale listings, because China requires a licensed coordinate system Google doesn't participate in. Use Apple Maps for actual navigation.

Does Apple Maps really work in China without a VPN?

Yes. Apple partners with Amap for licensed, correctly-aligned China data, and Apple's services operate inside China. Accuracy and freshness in mainland cities are excellent, with an English interface.

Why is Google's map of China offset?

Chinese law requires digital maps to use the GCJ-02 coordinate system, which applies an obfuscating transformation to true GPS coordinates. Licensed providers correct for it; Google, unlicensed in China, displays data that doesn't line up with your phone's real GPS position.

What about Google Maps in Hong Kong and Macau?

Fully accurate and unblocked. Both problems (the firewall and GCJ-02) apply to mainland China only.
