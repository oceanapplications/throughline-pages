Can you use Instagram in China?

travelersvpn.com guides

Not without help. Instagram has been blocked in mainland China since 2014, on every network including hotel wifi. The app opens but the feed never refreshes, posts won't upload, and DMs silently fail. It works normally in Hong Kong and Macau. To use it on the mainland, set up a VPN or a roaming eSIM before you arrive.

What it looks like when you land

Instagram doesn't tell you it's blocked. It opens, shows whatever it cached on the plane, and then spins. Stories won't load, the feed won't refresh, uploads hang at "Posting…", and DMs show as sent but never arrive. Plenty of travelers burn an afternoon rebooting their phone and re-installing the app before they realize nothing is broken except their location.

One more trap: if you delete the app while in China, you can't get it back. Instagram isn't on the mainland App Store, and reaching foreign app stores from inside is blocked too. Whatever you'll want on your phone has to be there before you cross the border.

The two ways travelers actually post from China

A VPN, installed before the flight. With a working tunnel, Instagram behaves completely normally: feed, Stories, Reels, DMs, uploads. The catch with the big commercial VPNs is that their shared servers are exactly what the firewall hunts, so connections are hit-or-miss and slow at peak hours. Traveler's VPN provisions a private server with an IP address only you use, so there's no shared endpoint on anyone's blocklist, and its routing sends Instagram, WhatsApp, and Gmail through the tunnel while WeChat, Alipay, Didi, and local maps stay direct and fast. That last part matters more than it sounds: with an all-or-nothing VPN you end up toggling it off to hail a taxi and forgetting to turn it back on for your Story.

A travel eSIM. Roaming data routes through gateways outside the firewall, so Instagram works on cellular with no VPN. The caveats: roughly $4/day for unlimited plans, and it only covers cellular. On hotel wifi you're behind the firewall again, which is exactly when most people settle in to post the day's photos. eSIM plus VPN is the combination that covers both.

For creators: photo posts survive a mediocre connection; long Reels uploads and live streams are what die. A private, uncongested server is the difference between "posted from Chengdu" and "posting when I'm back." Budget your upload sessions for when you're on solid wifi with the tunnel up.

What about TikTok, Facebook, and X?

Facebook and X (Twitter) are blocked the same way Instagram is, and behave the same through a VPN. TikTok is a different animal: it has never operated in mainland China (locals use Douyin, a separate ByteDance app with separate content), it's absent from mainland app stores, and it won't function on mainland networks. Travelers report mixed results using it even with a VPN because the app also checks things like SIM region. Have it installed before arrival and treat it as a maybe, not a promise.

Frequently asked

Does Instagram work in Hong Kong or Macau?

Yes, normally, with no VPN. The firewall covers mainland China only.

Why does my Instagram open but not load in China?

That's what the block looks like. The Great Firewall drops Instagram's traffic silently, so the app shows cached content and spins instead of erroring. Your phone and SIM are fine.

Can I post to Instagram from China with a VPN?

Yes. Through a working VPN, posting, Stories, Reels, and DMs all function normally. Large video uploads want a fast, uncongested connection, which is where a private server beats a crowded commercial one.

Will Instagram work on hotel wifi in China?

Not on its own; hotel wifi is filtered like every mainland network. With a VPN on, yes. A roaming eSIM doesn't help on wifi at all.
