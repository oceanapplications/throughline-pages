The real difference: who maintains the routing?

Respect where due: Shadowrocket is one of the great iOS power tools. It's been shipping since 2015, holds a 4.5-star average across twelve thousand ratings, and supports protocols most people have never heard of. If you enjoy network plumbing, it will never limit you.

But look at what "set it up for a trip" actually means. Out of the box, Shadowrocket gives you global proxy modes and a GeoIP database. The routing intelligence, which domains go direct, which go through your server, what to do with ads and trackers, comes from config files the community maintains on GitHub. You find one, judge whether it's trustworthy and current, import it, and repeat when it rots. The best guides, and most of the ecosystem, are in Chinese. That's a fine trade for a tinkerer. It's a terrible one the night before a flight.

Traveler's VPN makes the routing the product. The travel profiles are built in, based on the same battle-tested rule-set data the pros use (SagerNet's geosite/geoip, compiled for sing-box), and the app keeps them updated automatically. Paste your Outline key or WireGuard config and you're routing correctly in a minute, in English, with support if it doesn't work.

And there are two things Shadowrocket structurally doesn't offer. There's no managed tier: when a friend without a VPS asks "what do I do?", Shadowrocket has no answer, while Traveler's VPN provisions them a private server for $9.99. And there's no real Mac app: it runs as an iPad app on Apple Silicon only, while Traveler's VPN ships a native menu-bar Mac client in the same purchase.

What Shadowrocket genuinely does better

  • Protocol breadth. VLESS/Reality, Hysteria2, TUIC, Snell, SSH and more. If your server speaks something exotic, Shadowrocket speaks it too.
  • Rule-engine depth. Per-request rules, Lua scripting, URL rewriting, MITM inspection. Traveler's VPN deliberately doesn't expose this surface.
  • Subscription URLs. First-class support for bulk server imports from "airport" resellers.
  • Apple TV and Vision Pro. It runs on more Apple platforms than we do today.

Plenty of our BYO users graduated from Shadowrocket, and some power users will rightly stay with it. The question is just whether you want to operate the routing or have it operated for you.

Questions Shadowrocket users ask.

They're both $2.99. What am I actually choosing between?

Maintenance. Shadowrocket's $2.99 buys a rule engine you configure and maintain with community config files. Traveler's VPN's $2.99 BYO unlock buys routing profiles that are already built, self-update, and come with English documentation and support. Plus the option of a managed private server ($9.99/week or $14.99/month) that Shadowrocket has no equivalent for.

Can Shadowrocket use my Outline server?

Generally yes, since Outline keys are Shadowsocks URIs, though it isn't a first-class flow. Traveler's VPN parses Outline access keys, WireGuard configs, Shadowsocks URIs, and Trojan URIs directly, and tells you exactly what didn't parse if something's off.

Does either work on the Mac?

Traveler's VPN has a native menu-bar macOS app covered by the same Universal Purchase, on both Apple Silicon and Intel. Shadowrocket has no Mac build; its App Store listing is marked "Designed for iPad, Not verified for macOS," and it can only be installed on Apple Silicon Macs as an iPad app.

Is Shadowrocket's extra protocol support worth it?

If your server runs VLESS/Reality, Hysteria2, or another protocol we don't support, then yes, use Shadowrocket; it's the best client for that. If you run Outline, WireGuard, Shadowsocks, or Trojan (the most common self-hosted setups), Traveler's VPN covers you with far less configuration.
