The real difference: what "connected" does to your phone.

Connect NordVPN on your iPhone in Shanghai and every app on the phone now speaks from a foreign server. Didi thinks you're abroad. Alipay's risk checks get jumpy. Local maps and food delivery crawl through a round trip across the planet. Meanwhile your US bank sees a shared datacenter IP that hundreds of strangers also use, and treats you like a fraud case.

None of that is a bug in NordVPN. It's the design: one tunnel, everything through it. On Windows and Android, Nord lets you exclude apps. On iPhone and Mac, the platforms travelers actually carry, it offers nothing, so the real-world workflow becomes the toggle: on for Instagram, off for taxis, on for Gmail, off to pay. All day.

Traveler's VPN replaces the toggle with a routing table. Destination-aware profiles send local traffic direct and home traffic home, per connection, automatically, with the VPN connected the whole time. And because your server is private, provisioned for you alone, your banking app sees one consistent IP address that isn't on anyone's VPN blocklist.

There's also the pricing shape. Nord's famous low prices are intro rates on 1-2 year commitments billed up front, renewing at roughly 3-5x the intro monthly rate. Traveler's VPN charges for how travel actually happens: $9.99 for the trip, or a flat $14.99/month that never steps up.

What NordVPN genuinely does better

  • Scale. 9,000+ servers in 110+ countries and 10 simultaneous connections. If you need endpoints everywhere on every device type, Nord has them.
  • Speed. NordLynx retains ~95% of line speed in independent 2026 tests. Genuinely among the fastest.
  • Security extras. Threat Protection blocks ads, trackers, and malicious sites network-wide; repeated PwC and Deloitte no-logs audits; RAM-only servers; post-quantum encryption work.
  • Platform breadth. Windows, Linux, Android, TVs, routers. Traveler's VPN is Apple-only by design.

As an always-on security subscription, NordVPN earns its market share. As the thing that makes your phone work the week you're in Beijing or Tokyo, it's the wrong shape.

Questions travelers ask about NordVPN.

Does NordVPN have split tunneling on iPhone or Mac?

No. NordVPN's own support documentation lists app-based split tunneling on Windows, Android, and Android TV only (Linux gets a port/subnet allowlist). There is no split tunneling on iOS or macOS. Traveler's VPN does automatic per-destination routing on iPhone, iPad, and Mac.

Does NordVPN work in China?

It can, with effort. Standard connections are blocked; users must switch to obfuscated servers or the NordWhisper protocol, and recent traveler reports describe it as workable but needing more fiddling and server changes than China specialists. Everything must be set up before arrival since Nord's site is blocked there. Traveler's VPN takes the private-server approach: an IP address only you use, with China-specific routing profiles built in.

What does NordVPN really cost?

As of August 2026: $12.99 for a single month, or intro rates around $3-6/month that require paying for 1-2 years up front. The widely documented catch is renewal: long plans renew at roughly 3-5x the intro monthly rate. Traveler's VPN is $9.99 one-time for a 7-day trip, or a flat $14.99/month.

Can I get a refund if I subscribe on iPhone?

NordVPN's 30-day money-back guarantee excludes purchases made through the Apple App Store; those refunds go through Apple's own process, which is discretionary. Traveler's VPN avoids the problem: the 3-day trial charges nothing up front, and the Travel Pass never auto-renews.
