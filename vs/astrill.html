The real difference: what happens to your local apps.

Astrill answers one question extremely well: "can I reach the blocked internet from inside China?" It has answered it reliably for over a decade, which is why expats keep paying for it.

But a traveler has a second question Astrill's iPhone app doesn't answer: "do the apps I need in China keep working while I'm connected?" On iOS, Astrill has no per-app filtering. When the tunnel is up, your Didi ride-hailing, Alipay payments, Meituan orders, and local maps ride through a foreign server too, which makes them slow or breaks them outright. So you toggle. All day.

Traveler's VPN was built around that second question. The routing engine decides per destination: WeChat, Alipay, Didi, and local maps stay direct and fast, while WhatsApp, Instagram, YouTube, X, Gmail, and everything else the firewall blocks goes through the tunnel. Automatically, on iPhone, up to ~10x faster on local apps because their traffic never crosses the planet twice.

There's also the server model. Astrill puts you on shared infrastructure, and its best China routes (the CN2 "VIP" servers) cost from $10/month extra with a 100 GB cap. Traveler's VPN provisions a private server that only you use, included in the pass or subscription, with 1 TB of data.

What Astrill genuinely does better

  • Track record under pressure. Astrill has stayed usable through GFW crackdowns that took other commercial VPNs down. If your livelihood depends on connectivity in China year-round, that history is worth real money.
  • Purpose-built stealth protocols. StealthVPN and OpenWeb are engineered specifically for deep-packet-inspection evasion.
  • Premium China routing. The VIP add-on peers with China Telecom CN2 and China Unicom backbones for lower latency, something few competitors offer at any price.
  • Breadth of platforms. Linux, Windows, and router support. Traveler's VPN is Apple-only by design.

If that's what you need, Astrill is a fair choice. Most travelers don't need it; they need a week of their phone just working.

Questions travelers ask about Astrill.

Is Astrill really $30 a month?

Yes, as of August 2026 Astrill's monthly plan is $30, with a 1-year plan at $15/month ($180 billed up front) and a 2-year plan at $12.50/month ($300 up front). The VIP add-on for its premium Asia routes starts at another $10/month. Astrill's FAQ states all sales are final with no refunds.

Does Astrill's iPhone app have split tunneling?

No. Astrill's per-app Application Filter is available on desktop and Android only. On iOS, Astrill offers Smart Mode (website-region routing over its OpenWeb protocol) and a manual website list, but every app's traffic goes through the tunnel when connected. Traveler's VPN routes per destination automatically on iPhone, iPad, and Mac.

Which one is more reliable inside China?

For long-term residents, Astrill has the strongest track record of any commercial VPN and we won't pretend otherwise. Traveler's VPN takes a different approach: instead of shared commercial endpoints that censors target, you get a private server on an IP address only you use, which has no public reputation to block. For a trip, that private-server model plus smart routing is the better fit; for multi-year residency, redundancy matters and many expats keep two options installed.

Can I try Traveler's VPN before paying?

Yes. The app is free to download and the managed server starts with a 3-day free trial, no account and no payment up front. If the routing works for you, grab a $9.99 Travel Pass for the trip or keep the $14.99/month subscription. Astrill's 7-day trial requires an email account plus phone-number verification.
