The real difference: one switch vs a router.

ExpressVPN on iPhone is a beautifully made on/off switch. Flip it on and every packet, from every app, exits through a server in another country. That's exactly right for hiding your traffic on hotel Wi-Fi. It's exactly wrong for the daily texture of a trip: your ride-hailing app now thinks you're in Frankfurt, your local maps crawl, your payment QR code times out, and your banking app sees a datacenter IP and locks you out.

ExpressVPN knows this, which is why its iOS app offers split tunneling by individual IP address. Its own documentation warns the feature won't work reliably for large websites, and it can't exclude apps at all. So in practice, travelers toggle: VPN on for Instagram and Gmail, VPN off for taxis and payments, dozens of times a day.

Traveler's VPN starts from the toggle problem. The routing engine ships with per-destination profiles: local services stay direct and fast, while Instagram, WhatsApp, YouTube, Gmail, and the rest of the blocked half of your phone go through the tunnel, per connection, automatically. Your phone behaves like your phone, in both directions, with the VPN on the whole time.

The server model differs too. Instead of shared endpoints used by thousands of people (the reason banks challenge VPN logins, and the reason censors can blocklist commercial VPN ranges wholesale), each Travel Pass or subscription provisions a private server with an IP address that's yours alone.

What ExpressVPN genuinely does better

  • Raw speed and polish. Lightway is consistently among the fastest protocols tested, and the apps are mature on every platform.
  • Audit cadence. 23 independent audits since 2020, including repeated KPMG no-logs audits, plus RAM-only TrustedServer infrastructure. Best-in-class transparency for a commercial VPN.
  • Streaming. Reliably unblocks major streaming libraries; that's not what Traveler's VPN is for.
  • Coverage beyond Apple. Windows, Linux, Android, TVs, and its own Aircove router. Traveler's VPN is iPhone, iPad, and Mac only.

If you want a permanent, everything-everywhere privacy subscription, ExpressVPN is a strong one. If you want your apps to work on a two-week trip, that's our whole product.

Questions travelers ask about ExpressVPN.

Does ExpressVPN have split tunneling on iPhone?

Only by single IP address, not by app or domain. ExpressVPN's own support page notes it won't work reliably for large websites and that it's incompatible with the Advanced Kill Switch. Per-app split tunneling exists on Windows, Android, Linux, and (as of late 2025) the new Mac app. Traveler's VPN routes per destination automatically on iPhone, iPad, and Mac.

Which is cheaper for a single trip?

For one trip, Traveler's VPN's $9.99 non-renewing 7-day pass beats ExpressVPN's $12.99+ auto-renewing month. ExpressVPN's headline low prices (from around $2.49/month) require paying for one or two years up front and renew at roughly $99.95+/year. If you travel constantly and also want streaming and desktop coverage, that annual math can still work in ExpressVPN's favor.

Does ExpressVPN work in China?

Reports through 2025-2026 are mixed: it can work with the right server locations and protocol settings, but reviewers describe it as hit-or-miss and rank it behind China specialists. Its website is blocked there, so everything must be installed before arrival. Traveler's VPN provisions a private server with an IP only you use, which has no public VPN reputation to blocklist, and the app should likewise be installed before you land.

Is there a free trial?

Both offer one. ExpressVPN has a 7-day mobile trial that auto-converts to a paid subscription unless cancelled, plus a 30-day money-back guarantee you claim through support. Traveler's VPN's 3-day trial requires no account and no payment method up front; if you do nothing, you simply aren't charged.
