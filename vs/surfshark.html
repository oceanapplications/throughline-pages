The real difference: a bypass list you maintain vs routing that's done.

Surfshark deserves credit for trying: its Bypasser feature is split tunneling, and on Windows, Android, and even macOS it can exclude whole apps. But on iPhone, the device in your pocket when you land, Surfshark's own documentation is plain: only website bypassing is available. You can't tell it "leave my ride-hailing app alone." You'd have to know every domain your taxi app talks to, add each one by hand, and keep that list current. Nobody does that. They toggle.

Even where Bypasser works, it's a manual include/exclude list. There's no notion of "I'm traveling in China, route accordingly." Traveler's VPN ships that notion as its core: destination profiles route local traffic direct and home traffic home, per connection, automatically, on every Apple device you own.

Then there's the money. Surfshark's $2.49/month headline means $67.23 charged today for 27 months of service, renewing at $79/year. If a VPN is a permanent utility for your household, that's a fine deal. If it's for the two weeks a year you're abroad, you're either buying two years of something you need for two weeks, or paying ~$15.45 for an auto-renewing month you'll have to remember to cancel from a hotel room.

What Surfshark genuinely does better

  • Unlimited devices. One subscription covers every phone, laptop, and TV in the family. Nobody else in the premium tier does this.
  • Long-term price. $2.49/month on the 24-month intro term is the cheapest premium VPN going, even accounting for the $79/year renewal.
  • CleanWeb. Ad, tracker, and malware-domain blocking included on all plans.
  • Breadth. Fast WireGuard speeds, independent no-logs audits, 100+ countries, Windows/Android/Linux/TV apps, and macOS app-level Bypasser.

For a household's always-on VPN, Surfshark is a strong budget pick. For the trip in your calendar, it's the wrong unit of purchase.

Questions travelers ask about Surfshark.

Does Surfshark's Bypasser work on iPhone?

Only for websites. Surfshark's own support article states that only website bypassing is available in the iOS app; per-app exclusion exists on Windows, Android, and macOS but not iPhone. Traveler's VPN routes per destination automatically on iPhone, iPad, and Mac.

Isn't Surfshark much cheaper?

Per month on a 24-month contract, yes: $2.49/month intro, renewing at $79/year. For a single trip, no: a Surfshark month is ~$15.45 and auto-renews, while a Traveler's VPN Travel Pass is $9.99 once, with a private server included and nothing to cancel afterward.

Does Surfshark work in China?

Sometimes. Its NoBorders obfuscation mode helps, and independent 2026 testing measured roughly 65% connection success, workable for messaging but frustrating for video. Reviewers position it as a budget option for China rather than a reliable one. Traveler's VPN uses a private server with an IP address only you use, plus China-specific routing profiles; either way, install before you land.

What about my banking app?

Surfshark itself acknowledges banks flag its shared IPs and suggests either its dedicated-IP add-on (~$3.75/month) or manually bypassing your bank's website, which on iPhone doesn't help the bank's app. With Traveler's VPN, your banking app either stays direct (seeing your real local connection) or exits through a private IP only you use, so there's no shared-VPN fingerprint to flag.
